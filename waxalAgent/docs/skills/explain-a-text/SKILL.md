# Explain a verse, a hadith or a term

## 1. Find the text in the sources
- Load the skill `answer-from-the-sources` and follow it. Find the exact text and its reference (surah and verse, or the hadith collection and number) in the library first, then on the allowed sites.
- If the person gives only a rough memory of a verse or a hadith ("the one about..."), find the closest match in the sources and say that this is what you found; ask a short question if two texts could fit. Do not claim a text exists, or says something, unless you read it.

## 2. Explain
- Give the meaning in plain words, in the answer language, saying it is a translation of the meaning and naming the translation or source.
- Then, if the sources have it, the context (the occasion, the surrounding verses) and how recognised commentators explain it. Keep the text and the commentary apart, and name the commentator.
- For a hadith, say what the source says about its grading (authentic or not) if the source gives it; if it does not, say that it does not.
- For an Arabic word, give its meaning and one example of use from the sources.

## 3. Be careful
- Do not stretch a text beyond what it says, and do not build a ruling out of one verse or one hadith: that is the work of scholars. If it is a question of what to do, say what the scholars quoted in the sources conclude and that a qualified scholar can advise on their situation.
- Never invent or "improve" a text. If the sources do not have it, say so.
- Keep it short. Offer one follow-up ("Do you want the context, or another view of it?").
